I · PROLOGUE

One fire. Six pieces.

Before the first night there was one fire. Then it was cut into six, and the pieces were scattered along a road that runs from the western edge of the world to a door no one has opened.

The shards burn on somewhere in the dark — in a forest that grows on ash, beneath the water of a city that stopped to wait, in a court of black glass where no fire burns. At the western end of the road, a single hearth has burned since that first night.

Carry it east. Break what stands in your way. Bring the fire home.

“A fire was cut into six pieces and scattered along the road.”

The design

How the pilgrimage works

Three loops turn inside one another: the walk across a pilgrimage, the turn inside a fight, and the slow change of a deck and its flame. This is how each of them works.

Loop one · the pilgrimage

Every step is a choice

A pilgrimage crosses three acts. Each act is a map of fifteen waystones woven from six paths, and you choose every step: which fights to take, which mysteries to risk, when to rest and when to press on.

  • Paths split and rejoin. A safer road now can mean a harder one later. Elites guard the best rewards.
  • Unlit waystones hide what they hold, but pay a bounty to whoever lights them.
  • A fire before every boss. The last waystone of each act is always a rest site: heal, or reforge one card.

Loop two · the turn

Five cards, three candles, one lantern

Energy refills every turn. Embers stay. Each card you feed to the lantern is gone for the rest of the fight — so every turn asks what you can afford to burn.

  1. DrawFive cards come to hand.
  2. LightThree candles of Energy pay for what you play.
  3. StrikeCards spend Energy to hit, guard and afflict.
  4. KindleOnce a turn, feed any card to the lantern for one Ember.
  5. ArtOnce a turn, spend Embers on a Lantern Art.
  6. ReadEvery foe shows its intent before it acts.

Loop two · the glass

Chip. Crack. Shatter.

Every creature is glass with a facet gauge: four facets for most foes, more for elites and bosses. An unblocked hit chips one facet. Fill the gauge and the glass shatters.

  • Shatter is a turn won. A shattered foe skips its next action, so timing the break against a big intent is the best defence there is.
  • Breaking feeds the fire. Every shatter spills two Embers. Glass you break pays for the Art that breaks the next one.
  • The glass learns. Each break makes the next one harder: the foe’s facet gauge grows by one.

Loop three · the deck

The flame reads your deck

Before each fight the lantern weighs the coloured glass in your deck and settles on a flame. It never watches how you play — only what you carry — and the fight keeps that flame to the end.

  • Clear glass is fuel, not noise. Cards with no colour never dim the flame. Cutting off-colour glass steadies it.
  • Lit glass. Some cards carry a rider that resolves only while the lantern burns their colour, Steady or True.
  • Ways are found, not chosen. The Duskblade’s glass leans three ways. Draft into one and the flame turns its colour.
